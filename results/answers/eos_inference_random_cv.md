No, random cross-validation is not sufficient to claim EOS inference from supernova gravitational waves.

According to Mitra et al. (2026), standard random cross-validation on raw waveforms yields misleadingly high performance due to template leakage [literature/md/mitra2026_generalization.md:L29-L29 § Introduction]. Template leakage occurs when many waveforms share the same EoS or progenitor model but differ in rotation profile, causing random splits to place closely related templates in both training and test sets [literature/md/mitra2026_generalization.md:L29-L29 § Introduction]. This allows the model to memorize geometric manifolds of specific templates rather than learning transferable physical correlations [literature/md/mitra2026_generalization.md:L29-L29 § Introduction].

Under Leave One EoS Out (LOEO) validation, where all waveforms from one EoS are withheld from training, the same models fail dramatically [literature/md/mitra2026_generalization.md:L17-L17 § The Generalization Gap in Machine Learning EoS Inference from Core-Collapse Supernova Gravitational Waves > Abstract]. For instance, a LightGBM regressor that appears successful under random CV with $R^2=(0.70,0.67,0.60)$ for $(K_0,J,L)$ fails under LOEO with mean absolute errors of $(44.57,3.19,30.54)$ MeV and negative pooled $R^2$ values, indicating performance worse than predicting the mean target value [literature/md/mitra2026_generalization.md:L17-L17 § The Generalization Gap in Machine Learning EoS Inference from Core-Collapse Supernova Gravitational Waves > Abstract]. This failure persists across linear models, random forests, neural networks, and gradient boosted trees [literature/md/mitra2026_generalization.md:L17-L17 § The Generalization Gap in Machine Learning EoS Inference from Core-Collapse Supernova Gravitational Waves > Abstract].

Even restricting inputs to low dimensional physical features like bounce amplitude, bounce width, and post bounce peak frequency reduces template leakage but does not restore reliable out of catalogue EoS inference [literature/md/mitra2026_generalization.md:L17-L17 § The Generalization Gap in Machine Learning EoS Inference from Core-Collapse Supernova Gravitational Waves > Abstract]. The study concludes that machine learning can interpolate within current CCSN waveform catalogues but this does not imply robust physical inference for unseen EoS models [literature/md/mitra2026_generalization.md:L17-L17 § The Generalization Gap in Machine Learning EoS Inference from Core-Collapse Supernova Gravitational Waves > Abstract].

Therefore, future studies should report Leave One EoS Out or leave family out validation, with MAE and mean predictor baselines, to verify physical generalisability [literature/md/mitra2026_generalization.md:L303-L303 § Discussion and Conclusions].
